    Layout edit mode¶

    Some fixes are not worth asking an agent for: a width, or a button outlined instead of filled. Layout edit mode lets you make those yourself, on the app's own screen.

    How a round goes¶

    1. Enter the mode¶

    Press Ctrl+Shift+E (Cmd+Shift+E on macOS). The mode's name and H help show at the centre for a moment. Inside comment mode the same chord switches over, your marks kept. Ctrl+Shift+C switches back.

    2. Select the widget¶

    Hover, and the widget under the cursor gets teal corner brackets and a caption naming its type. Hovering a button's label brackets the button. Click to select the widget. The selection stays until you click another widget or click nothing. It keeps its brackets while the pointer is on it, over its children too. Once the pointer leaves, a faint pair of brackets stays. When the container is what you want and its children cover every pixel of it, select a child and press W: W moves the selection to its parent, S back down.

    3. Drag a corner¶

    Drag a corner bracket. A dashed ghost follows the pointer, captioned with the value that will be written (w 240 | h auto). Esc before the release cancels the drag. The corner is one way to change the widget; its body, its handles and the bar over it are the others, each listed under Everything you can change in place of this step.

    4. Release, and read the badge¶

    Every drag ends on release. A pick ends on its row. The value is written into the call that built the widget. Hot reload applies it. The ghost stays until the reload lands. When one call builds many widgets, a helper returning a card called from a loop, every one of them gets a ghost, and the caption counts them (14 widgets). After the reload, a notice at the window's bottom-left, the badge, reports anything that did not go as the ghost said: a size that landed elsewhere, or a reload that failed on the edit.

    5. Undo if it went wrong¶

    Ctrl+Z reverts the last edit this mode wrote. The one exception is a line you have edited by hand since: the undo is refused. Ctrl+Shift+Z redoes what Ctrl+Z undid. A new edit clears what could be redone.

    6. Leave¶

    Press Esc. With a widget selected, the first press drops the selection and its bar; the second leaves.

    Everything you can change¶

    Each way below takes the place of step 3 of the round. The body's drag depends on its direction: along the container's axis it reorders, across it aligns, into another container it moves.

    Resize: drag a corner¶

    Drag a corner bracket. A dashed ghost follows the pointer, captioned with the value that will be written (w 240 | h auto), one of the three sizes a widget accepts:

    • "auto" when you release within a few pixels of the widget's natural size;
    • "wt" when you release where a weight would land: at a Column's full width, or at the share of the leftover a weight gets along the main axis; the caption reads wt there;
    • the integer otherwise.

    A widget with a size parameter (Icon) stays square: the larger of the two deltas wins.

    Hold Alt to land on an exact size: the pointer's pixel count is written instead of a snapped value. W / A / S / D while dragging a corner move it by one pixel. From the first press the mouse no longer moves it. auto / wt are then written only when the corner lands exactly on them.

    Reorder, or move into another container¶

    Drag the body along the container's axis to reorder among its siblings, or into another container to move it there. To drag a container whose children cover it, press in its padding, away from the midpoints of its edges. The container it would land in, its own or another under the pointer, is tinted. An insertion line marks the slot, captioned with the sibling it goes before. A "wt" moves as written. The badge notes when its meaning changed with the move: a "wt" that filled a Column's width becomes a share of a Row's leftover.

    In a Column, a Row, a Flow or a UniformFlow¶

    Up and down reorder in a Column, left and right in a Row. In a Flow or UniformFlow, any travel that leaves the widget's slot reorders.

    Into or out of a Grid¶

    The cell under the pointer is tinted instead of a line. Its caption names the row and column, or the area name, and whoever already sits there. A widget that leaves a Grid leaves its GridItem behind, and the badge names the width / height / padding / alignment the item carried.

    Over a Stack¶

    A list beside the stack names its layers, bottom to top, with the landing marked. A widget dragged in from outside lands in the top layer: inside it when the layer is a container, otherwise on top of the stack. It never lands in the background box underneath. A widget that started inside the stack stays in its own layer wherever the pointer is, so a card under a floating button still reorders in its column. W / S step the landing to another layer, and a digit 0–9 picks one by its number in the list. Past the top layer is a new one on top. The choice holds until the pointer leaves the stack.

    Into or out of a Container or a Box¶

    An empty box takes a widget dropped on it as its child. A box that already has a child is passed over, and the drop lands in the list around it. A widget that leaves a box leaves it behind, empty.

    Align¶

    Drag the body across the container's axis: left and right in a Column, up and down in a Row. In a Flow or UniformFlow, a drag that stays in the widget's slot aligns. In a Flow, it must be mostly up or down; mostly sideways within the slot, it does nothing. In a Stack, a Container or a Box, a drag in any direction aligns. The widget snaps to start / center / end on each axis. A dashed rect marks where every child of the container will land. The caption names the value (center, bottom-right) and how many children move. The alignment written is the container's. A CrossAligned you wrote yourself is respected: dragging its child rewrites that value. Other children with their own CrossAligned stay put when the container's alignment changes.

    Space it: the handles¶

    A hovered widget that takes a padding has an outlined pill, a handle, on each of its four edges. A Column or Row has one more in each gap between its children. Hover a handle and it fills, along with every handle that moves with it.

    Drag a handle¶

    Pull a padding handle away from the content and the padding grows. The content's edge is zero; pushing past it stays at zero. Push a gap handle toward the next child, down in a Column or right in a Row, and the gap grows. Every gap handle moves together, and every later child is drawn where the new gap puts it. A tinted band shows the value, from the content's edge out to the handle. Its caption is what will be written (padding 20, gap 12). Values snap to multiples of 4. Alt and W / A / S / D work as on a corner.

    On an axis the widget sizes to its content, the band grows outward and the handle rides with it. On a fixed width or height, a "wt", or a root that fills its window, the band grows inward from the edge instead: the content moves, and the handle does not follow the pointer.

    Click a handle¶

    Click a handle and the fill turns: every side moves as one (padding=20), then the pair the handle belongs to (padding=(20, 8)), then the one side alone (padding=(8, 8, 20, 8)), and round again. The shape starts as the source spells it.

    Pick a preset: the bar over the selection¶

    Select a widget whose style has presets, a button of any kind, a card, a chip, a text field or a slider among them, or a Text, and a bar appears just over it. The bar carries the widget's type, then a box for the preset and one for each argument the preset takes: style and size for a button, style alone for a card, type scale for a text. Each box shows what the source holds. Click a box and its list opens under it. Click a row to write it. W / S or ↑ / ↓ move along the list, Enter writes, Esc closes it.

    A box reads filled (default) when the call passes no style; a pick inserts the keyword. It reads custom when the value is not a preset, such as a copy_with(...), a variable or a constructor. A pick then replaces that whole expression.

    Edit the text¶

    Enter, or a second click on the selection, edits its text in place. A field opens over the widget. Enter writes the new string into the literal the code passed (text= / label=, or the first argument). Esc or a click elsewhere cancels. The field edits like a text field: Shift+←/→ selects, Ctrl+A/C/X/V (Cmd on macOS) select all, copy, cut and paste. Text that comes from a variable, an f-string or a function call cannot be edited here — the badge says so.

    Delete¶

    Delete / Backspace removes the selected widget from the source — its whole expression, children included. No confirmation. A handler or import the widget used stays behind for you or the agent to clean up.

    Every gesture¶

    Gesture What it does
    Ctrl+Shift+E Enter layout edit mode (Cmd+Shift+E on macOS). Inside comment mode it switches directly, your marks kept; Ctrl+Shift+C switches back.
    Hold H Show every gesture of this mode while the key is down.
    Hover The widget under the cursor gets teal corner brackets, a caption naming its type, and handles on its edges and in its gaps.
    Click Select it. W / S then move to its parent and back. The selection stays until another click or Esc.
    Drag a corner bracket Resize. A dashed ghost follows the pointer, captioned with the value that will be written.
    Drag a handle Change the padding on that side, or the gap. A band shows the value; away from the content is more.
    Click a handle Turn how many sides move with it: every side, its pair, or itself alone.
    W / A / S / D while dragging a corner or a handle Move it by one pixel; the mouse no longer moves it.
    Alt while dragging Land on the exact pixel count instead of snapping.
    Drag the body Reorder among its siblings, move into the container or grid cell under the pointer, or, keeping its place, align it.
    W / S / 0–9 while dragging over a Stack Pick the layer the widget lands in; past the top layer is a new one.
    Release Write and reload. The ghost stays until the reload lands.
    Click a box on the bar over the selection Open its list of presets; a row writes. W / S move along it, Enter writes, Esc closes.
    Enter, or a second click on the selection Edit its text in place; Enter writes, Esc or a click elsewhere cancels.
    Delete / Backspace Remove the selected widget from the source, children included.
    Ctrl+Z Undo the last edit this mode wrote.
    Ctrl+Shift+Z Redo what Ctrl+Z undid. A new edit clears what could be redone.
    Esc Cancel the drag in flight, or close the open list; otherwise drop the selection; with none, leave.

    Everything it writes, refuses, or ignores¶

    What it writes¶

    Layout edit mode writes these, and nothing else:

    • width, height and size;
    • a child's place in the list that orders the children — children=[...], or the data a comprehension or Column.builder() iterates, even one bound to a name (tags = [...]);
    • a GridItem's row / column or area, with the wrapper itself added or removed as a widget enters or leaves a Grid;
    • a container's alignment / cross_alignment / item_alignment, or a CrossAligned's value;
    • padding and gap, in the shape the handles stand for: one int, an (h, v) pair, or four sides;
    • style as a preset call (nv.ButtonStyle.outlined("m")), and type_scale as a role (nv.TypeScale.TITLE_LARGE); a module that imports Button itself gets ButtonStyle.outlined("m").

    It does not write colours, shapes or the other values inside a style.

    What it refuses¶

    The badge says why, and the file is left alone. That happens for:

    • Resize
    • a size bound to a name or an expression (width=self.card_w);
    • a widget whose constructor takes no width, height or size;
    • Reorder
    • a reorder whose order is not in one list literal in the file — head + tail, [first, *rest], a filtered comprehension, items from a call or a view model, a name that is assigned twice or used again after its list. The badge names the list;
    • a move out of, or into, a container whose children are not written as a list literal — Column.builder(), a ForEach, a comprehension. The badge says so while you hover, before you release;
    • a GridItem whose row / column or area name is bound to a name or an expression; it can neither change cell nor leave the grid;
    • a move into a Grid written as a bare Grid(...) in a module that does not import GridItem: no import is added, and the badge asks you for one;
    • Align
    • an alignment / cross_alignment / item_alignment bound to a name or an expression;
    • a widget as big as its container on every axis it could align on: "wt", or the same size;
    • Space
    • a padding or gap bound to a name, an expression or an observable;
    • Pick
    • a style or type_scale that may come through **kwargs;
    • a bare Button(...) in a module that does not import ButtonStyle (likewise Card / CardStyle, Text / TypeScale): no import is added, and the badge asks you for one;
    • Any
    • a widget whose call has moved or changed since the last reload, or one built by code outside your files.

    What does nothing¶

    No badge, no change:

    • Reorder
    • releasing the widget in its own slot, or its own grid cell;
    • releasing over a Grid's padding, where there is no cell;
    • Align
    • releasing on the alignment the container already has;
    • Space
    • releasing a handle where it started, which is a click: it turns the shape instead;
    • Pick
    • choosing the row the source already holds.
